Many proposals for the marine ecosystem’s regionalization and boundaries in the South Pacific Ocean (SPO) have been hypothesized. However, the lack of a consensus could reflect the different spatial scales and factors implicated in these proposals. In this research, we have described the epipelagic Marine Ecosystem regionalization to propose effective biodiversity conservation measures based on novel and integrative biogeographic modelling approaches using extensive databases to evaluate the relative importance of oceanographic and spatial processes at the SPO; including a combination of depth layers (0, 100, 200 m) to integrate the depth dimension. Its analyses encompassed a broad range of physical/biogeochemical and biological regimes and their interactions, shaping the number and boundaries of ecosystems within the region. The main novelties of this proposal include the use of Sea Level Anomalies, Eddy Kinetic Energy, and Upwelling index as environmental predictors as well as, for the first time, a combination of nutrient concentrations and their ratios has emphasized potential nutrient limitation to primary production in the SPO. A total of eight ecosystems were identified for the region, including coastal and oceanic systems. The distribution of chl-a biomass in the subtropical oceanic area is positively associated with NO3/Si values. A subsurface chl-a maximum at 200 m depth in the eastern boundary of the South Pacific gyre area (20-30°S, 90-120°W) is an exception. This maximum could be related to a minimum difference between the depth of the euphotic zone and the mixed layer depth. This is currently a more quantitative assessment of the relationship between physically and chemically distinct regions in the ocean, which could facilitate a deeper understanding of the ecological nature of marine ecosystems to take the next step in marine conservation policy in the SPO.
