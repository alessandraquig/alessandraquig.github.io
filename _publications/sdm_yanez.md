excerpt: 'Plankton play a central role in marine food webs and carbon sequestration, serving as vital bioindicators. Plankton biodiversity is also a key component of natural ecosystems, mediating essential ecosystem functions. Understanding the drivers of its  spatial patterns is a fundamental challenge for marine research, particularly  in the Southeast Pacific Ocean (SPO), a region critical to global climate regulation due to the scale of its biogeochemical and physical processes. The Intergovernmental Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES) highlighted the need to  promote knowledge generation and data management on biodiversity and ecosystem services as a foundation for informed decision-making. While various ocean-biogeographic units have been proposed for the SPO, the interactions between ecological components and environmental variables remains poorly characterized. In this context, it is essential to increase efforts to describe marine ecosystems and their biodiversity patterns, and to identify environmental drivers that shape them. This study integrates databases with ecological and biogeochemical datasets and applies probabilistic analyses based on niche theory, using  Bayesian species distribution models to assess the relationship between environmental variability and plankton diversity in the SPO. The findings contribute to a better understanding of how biodiversity responds to environmental gradients and climate variability, with important implications for assessing the impacts of global change at a spatially explicit regional scale.'
